# Batch 04 Discovery (2026-01-23 .. 2026-03-27) — Model: gemini-3.6-flash

Here is an objective linguistic analysis of the 10 parallel Arabic–Czech Friday sermon pairs from the Prague Mosque published between January and March 2026.

---

### 1. STRUCTURE & FRAMING

* **Standardized Header Preamble in Czech:**
  The Arabic source texts begin directly with their respective topic introductions or direct address. By contrast, **every single Czech text** inserts a standardized structural frame containing:
  1. The Gregorian date alongside the calculated Hijri date in parentheses (e.g., `Páteční kázání 27. 3. 2026 (8. šawwál 1447 h)` `[2026-03-27]`).
  2. A fixed opening sermon formula (*Khutbat al-Hajah*):
     > *"Chvála Alláhu. Jeho uctíváme a Jeho o pomoc prosíme. Dosvědčuji, že není božstva kromě Alláha a že Muhammad (salla lláhu ʻalajhi wa sallam) je Jeho služebník a posel. „Vy, kteří jste uvěřili, chraňte se před trestem Alláha a říkejte slova správná! On napraví vám vaše skutky a odpustí vám vaše hříchy; a ten, кто poslechne Alláha a posla Jeho, ten dosáhne nesmírného vítězství.“"*

  This preamble is completely absent from the written Arabic texts across all 10 sermons (e.g., `[2026-02-13]`, `[2026-02-06]`, `[2026-01-23]`).

* **Closing Supplications (*Du'ā'*):**
  Closing supplications are consistently rendered into Czech prose rather than left transliterated. 
  * In `[2026-03-27]`, the Arabic concludes with: 
    > AR: `اللهم ارزقنا السير على خطى أصحاب نبيك...`
    > CS: *"Alláhu, učiň nás upřímnými a nedopusť, abychom patřili k těm, kdo jsou lhostejní..."*
  * In `[2026-01-30]`, the Arabic closing supplication is translated closely:
    > AR: `وأسألُ اللهَ العظيمَ ربَّ العرشِ العظيمِ أن يغفرَ لنا...`
    > CS: *"Prosím Všemohoucího Alláha, Pána Velkého trůnu, aby nám odpustil..."*

### 2. SCRIPTURE & CITATIONS

* **Quranic Citation Mechanics:**
  * **Surah & Verse Referencing:** The Czech translations systematically add parenthetical Surah and verse numbers `(Surah:Verse)` at the end of Quranic citations, even when the Arabic source text omits them completely.
    * Example `[2026-03-27]`: AR `أولئك الذين هدى الله فبهداهم اقتده` $\rightarrow$ CS *„A toto jsou ti, jež Alláh správně vedل – jdi tedy po správné cestě jejich!“ (6:90)*.
    * Example `[2026-03-20]`: AR `﴿إِنَّمَا يَتَقَبَّلُ اللَّهُ مِنَ الْمُتَّقِينَ﴾` $\rightarrow$ CS *„Alláh přijímá (oběť) jen od bohabojných.“ (5:27)*.
  * **Czech Quran Translation Style:** Quranic verses use an elevated, archaic Czech register featuring traditional inversions (e.g., *"trpěliví jste byli"* `[2026-03-27]`, *"My zajisté připomenutí jsme seslali"* `[2026-03-06]`).

* **Prophetic Hadiths & Honorific Blessings:**
  * The Arabic eulogy for the Prophet (`صلى الله عليه وسلم` / `ﷺ`) is uniformly rendered in Czech as standard Latin transliteration inside dashes: `– salla lláhu alajhi wa sallam –` or parenthetically `(salla lláhu ʻalajhi wa sallam)`.
  * Eulogies for Companions (`رضي الله عنه` / `رضي الله عنها`) are transliterated as `– radija lláhu anhu –` / `– radija lláhu anhá –` (e.g., Aisha in `[2026-03-20]`, `[2026-03-13]`).
  * Eulogies for deceased scholars (`رحمه الله`) are translated functionally into native Czech: `– nechť je k němu Alláh milosrdný –` (e.g., Sufyan al-Thawri and Imam Malik in `[2026-03-13]`).

### 3. AUDIENCE, TONE & RHETORICAL EDITING

* **Gender Inclusivity in Direct Address:**
  While the Arabic text uses traditional male-plural forms when addressing the congregation, the Czech version frequently broadens the terms to explicitly include both genders.
  * `[2026-03-20]`: AR `أيها الإخوة الكرام` ("O honorable brothers") $\rightarrow$ CS *"Milí bratři a sestry"*.
  * `[2026-02-27]`: AR `أيها المؤمنون` ("O believers") $\rightarrow$ CS *"Bratři a sestry"*.

* **Condensation and Paragraph Merging:**
  The Czech versions frequently streamline, merge, or abbreviate detailed jurisprudential lists present in the Arabic originals.
  * **Omission of Scholarly Details `[2026-01-30]`:** The Arabic text lists specific categories of legitimate social avoidance (*hajr*):
    > AR: `كهجر العاصي إذا كان فيه زجر وتأديب، أو هجر أهل البدع والفسوق إذا كانوا يفسدون الناس...`
    The Czech text condenses this entire scholarly exception down to a generic sentence, skipping the explicit references to sinners and heretics:
    > CS: *"Ačkoli vyhýbání se může být v některých případech přípustné z platného právního důvodu, základním principem pro muslimy je mít čisté srdce..."*
  * **Omission of Hadith Examples `[2026-02-06]`:** The Arabic sermon details how Prophet Muhammad prohibited Al-Hasan from eating a date meant for charity (`نهى الحسن رضي الله عنه عن تمرة الصدقة`). This detail is omitted entirely from the Czech translation.

  > CS: *"...jenž učinil obchod na tomto světě cestou k posmrtnému životu."*

### 5. SURPRISES, EDGE CASES & HUMAN QUIRKS

* **Orthographic Typo in Calendar Dates:**
  In four consecutive sermons (`[2026-02-13]`, `[2026-02-06]`, `[2026-01-30]`, `[2026-01-23]`), the Czech header features a character composition error in the transliteration of the Hijri month *Sha'ban*:
  > Written as: `š´abán` (containing a standalone acute accent mark `´` before the `a`, instead of an apostrophe `š'abán` or `ša'bán`).

* **Literal vs. Functional Hadith Classification Translation:**
  In `[2026-02-20]`, the Arabic introduces a Hadith Qudsi with standard wording: `قال ﷺ فيما يرويه عن ربه:` (*"The Prophet ﷺ said in what he narrates from his Lord:"*). The Czech translator uses the established formal Islamic studies terminology:
  > CS: *"V posvátném hadísu Alláh praví..."* (*"In a sacred hadith Allah says..."*).

* **Header Date Realignment:**
  In sermon `[2026-03-20]`, the Czech header notes `1. šawwál 1447 h` (marking Eid al-Fitr), matching the content of the sermon which discusses the completion of Ramadan and signs of accepted deeds (`Jak se pozná, že byly zbožné skutky přijaty?`).
