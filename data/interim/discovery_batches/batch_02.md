### 1. STRUCTURE & FRAMING

* **Asymmetric Framing & Standardized Openings:**
  * **Czech Text Standardized Frame:** Every Czech sermon in this batch begins with a fixed header format containing the Gregorian date, Hijri date, title, followed by a standardized rendition of *Khutbat al-Hājah* (Opening Sermon Formula) and Quran 33:70–71:
    > *„Páteční kázání DD. M. YYYY (DD. [month] 1448 h )“*  
    > *„Chvála Alláhu. Jeho uctíváme a Jeho o pomoc prosíme. Dosvědčuji, že není božstva kromě Alláha a že Muhammad (salla lláhu ʻalajhi wa sallam) je Jeho služebník a posel. ‚Vy, kteří jste uvěřili, chraňte se před trestem Alláha a říkejte slova správná! On napraví vám vaše skutky a odpustí vám vaše hříchy; a ten, kdo poslechne Alláha a posla Jeho, ten dosáhne nesmírného vítězství.‘“*
  * **Arabic Transcripts:** The written Arabic texts **omit** this standardized opening ritual entirely and jump directly into the main thematic introduction of the sermon (e.g., `مع بداية العام الدراسي الجديد` `[2026-08-28]`, `يستقبل المسلمون في شهر ربيع الأول` `[2026-08-21]`, `قد يستطيع الإنسان أن يختفي` `[2026-08-14]`).

* **Dates & Titles:**
  * Czech titles incorporate explicit Hijri dates in the header (e.g., `15. rabí´al-awwal 1448 h` `[2026-08-28]`, `24. safar 1448 h` `[2026-08-07]`, `18. muharram 1448 h` `[2026-07-03]`).
  * Arabic titles omit Hijri dates, appending only the Gregorian date in parentheses at the end of the title line (e.g., `(2026/8/21)`).

* **Paragraphing & Transition Markers:**
  * Direct vocative transitions in Arabic (`عباد الله،`, `أيها الآباء والأمهات،`, `أيها المسلمون،`, `أيها الأبناء والبنات،`) are rendered in Czech as standalone paragraph breaks (`Služebníci Alláhovi,`, `Otcové a matky,` / `Vážení rodiče,`, `Muslimové,`, `Synové a dcery,`).
  * In several sermons (notably `[2026-08-28]` and `[2026-08-14]`), Czech combines multiple shorter Arabic paragraphs into dense text blocks.

* **Closing Supplications (Duʿāʾ):**
  * Closing prayers are translated faithfully. In `[2026-06-26]`, the Czech text appends an explicit `Ámín.` at the end of the closing prayer, which does not appear in the written Arabic transcript.

### 2. SCRIPTURE & CITATIONS

* **Quranic Citations:**
  * **Format:** Arabic includes full verse text in brackets with Surah names and numbers (e.g., `﴿اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ﴾[العلق: 1]`). Czech uses standard Czech translation conventions (Ivan Hrbek style) with simple numerical chapter-verse brackets, omitting Surah names (e.g., `„Přednášej ve jménu Pána svého, který stvořil…“ (96:1)` `[2026-08-28]`).
  * **Citation Errors in Translation:** In `[2026-07-24]`, Arabic cites `﴿لَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا أَشْرَكْنَا﴾ [الأنعام: 148]`. The Czech text mislabels the verse number as `(6:143)`.

* **Prophetic Hadiths & Eulogies:**
  * Transliterated honorifics are consistently inserted into Czech prose:
    * **Prophet Muhammad:** `– salla lláhu alajhi wa sallam –` or `(salla lláhu ʻalajhi wa sallam)`.
    * **Companions:** `– radija lláhu anhu –` / `– radija lláhu anhumá –` / `– radija lláhu anhum –`.
    * **Other Prophets:** `– alajhi as-salám –` / `– alajhim as-salám –`.
  * **Hadith Source Notes:** Arabic explicitly cites formal hadith collection references in prose (e.g., `(صحيح مسلم٤/ 2295)` `[2026-08-07]`), which are systematically stripped out of the Czech translated text.
