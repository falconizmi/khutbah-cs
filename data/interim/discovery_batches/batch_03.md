### 1. STRUCTURE & FRAMING
*   **Titles:** Arabic titles are typically poetic or thematic (e.g., `في رحاب شهر الله المحرم` — "In the Expansive Realms of Allah’s Month Muharram" `[2026-06-19]`), whereas Czech titles are often descriptive or explanatory, sometimes expanding on the Arabic (e.g., Arabic: `الهجرة النبوية: مدرسة التوكل والأخذ بالأسباب` vs. Czech: `Prorokův odchod z Mekky do Medíny: lekce o spoléhání se na Alláha a vykonávání všeho, co je v našich silách` `[2026-06-12]`).
*   **Openings:** While the Arabic texts begin directly with thematic praise or rhetorical hooks (e.g., `إن من نعم الله تعالى...` `[2026-06-19]`), **all** Czech texts uniformly insert the standard Prague Mosque Khutbah opening formula: *"Chvála Alláhu. Jeho uctíváme a Jeho o pomoc prosíme. Dosvědčuji, že není božstva kromě Alláha..."* followed by the Qur'anic verse of *taqwa* (Surah Al-Imran 3:102: *"Vy, kteří jste uvěřili, chraňte se před trestem Alláha..."*), which does not appear in the Arabic source texts provided.
*   **Transitions & Address:** Czech translations frequently insert conventional Khutbah vocatives like *"Vážení bratři a sestry"* (Respected brothers and sisters) `[2026-05-15]`, which adapt the slightly more varied Arabic addresses (`أيها الإخوة الكرام`, `أيها الأحبة`).
*   **Closings:** Both languages conclude with supplications (*du'a*). The Czech texts closely mirror the Arabic petitions, typically opening with *"Prosíme Alláha Všemohoucího..."* `[2026-05-22]` or *"Ó Alláhu..."* `[2026-05-15]`.

### 2. SCRIPTURE & CITATIONS
*   **Quranic Verses:** 
    *   *Arabic:* Framed with Qur'anic brackets (`﴿...﴾`) and accompanied by surah and verse references in brackets, e.g., `[يوسف: 111]` `[2026-06-19]`.
    *   *Czech:* Integrated into the running prose in quotation marks, with references converted to standard academic colon notation in parentheses, e.g., `(12:111)` `[2026-06-19]` or `(3:190)` `[2026-04-24]`.
*   **Hadiths:** 
    *   *Arabic:* Enclosed in guillemets (`«...»`) and attributed with traditional phrasing like `رواه مسلم` `[2026-06-19]`.
    *   *Czech:* Enclosed in standard Czech quotation marks (`"..."`) with source notes parenthetical and localized, e.g., `(podle Muslima)` `[2026-06-19]`.
*   **Prophetic and Sacred Names:** Abbreviations or embedded honorifics in Arabic (`ﷺ`, `عليه السلام`) are systematically expanded in Czech into hyphenated transliterated formulas set off by em-dashes or parentheses, such as `(salla lláhu ʻalajhi wa sallam)` `[2026-06-19]`, `– alajhi as-salám –` `[2026-06-19]`, and `– radija lláhu anhu –` `[2026-06-12]`.

### 3. AUDIENCE, TONE & RHETORICAL EDITING

Rhetorical questions in Arabic are sometimes restructured into direct statements or inclusive first-person plurals in Czech (e.g., Arabic: `وقد يتساءل البعض...` -> Czech: *"Můžeme se ptát..."* / "We can ask..." `[2026-06-19]`).

For example, in `[2026-04-03]`, the Arabic text contains a highly structured, multi-tiered bulleted list (`1- الحب الصادق وإظهاره...` with sub-bullets for *الفرد، الأسرة، المجتمع*). The Czech version flattens and condenses these structured items into dense running paragraphs, omitting the granular contextual sub-categories.
